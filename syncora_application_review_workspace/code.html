Statement of Purpose

Growing up in a community where technological resources were scarce, my initial exposure to computer science was through a refurbished desktop in our local library. That singular machine became my gateway to understanding logic structures, problem-solving, and ultimately, the realization that software could scale solutions to systemic issues.

During my undergraduate studies, I focused heavily on algorithmic efficiency and data structures. However, my most formative experience occurred during my junior year internship at the City Data Initiative. Tasked with optimizing transit route schedules, I realized that elegant code alone is insufficient; it must be coupled with an empathetic understanding of the end-user. Our model, while mathematically sound, initially failed to account for localized traffic anomalies near public schools—a realization that shifted my entire paradigm toward human-centered computational design.

I am applying to the Horizon Merit Scholarship to fund my graduate research in developing adaptive machine learning models for civic infrastructure. My goal is to create systems that do not merely process data, but interpret it through the lens of community equity. The financial support from this fund will allow me to dedicate my full attention to this research, rather than splitting my time with part-time employment.
